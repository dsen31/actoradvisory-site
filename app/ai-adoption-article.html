Your Team Has AI Tools. So Why Is Nobody Using Them?

November 16, 2026

A company buys AI licenses. Everyone gets a login. Leadership sends a nice email. A month later, two people use it every day, and everyone else opened it twice and went back to their old way of working.

If that sounds like your company, you are not alone, and it is not your team's fault. For a leader, it also means you are paying for licenses that are not paying you back. The tool is not the problem. The training is.

Why AI rollouts stall

  • Nobody shows people what good looks like. "Just ask it anything" is not a lesson. Most people do not know what to ask, so they ask something small and move on.
  • People type vague questions and get vague answers. Then they decide AI is overhyped. The truth is that a vague question gets a vague answer from anyone, human or not.
  • It is not tied to real work. A sales rep needs help with account research and follow-ups. An engineer needs something totally different. One generic demo cannot cover both.
  • Nobody owns it. When AI is everyone's job, it ends up being no one's job. Leadership has to name one person to own the rollout and report on it, or interest fades after the first week.

Three habits that fix most of it

1. Give it a job and some context.

Think of it like onboarding a new coworker. They are smart, but they do not know your customers, your goals, or your style until you tell them.

Weak: "Write a follow-up email."

Better: "You are my sales partner. Write a short, friendly follow-up to a security director who saw our demo last week and went quiet. Keep it under 100 words and end with one easy question."

2. Treat the first answer like a rough draft.

Great results come from the second and third message. Say "shorter," "less salesy," or "add a real example." You are coaching it, not just asking it. People who stop after one try miss most of the value.

3. Save what works.

Anything you do every week, like call prep, meeting notes, or proposals, deserves a saved prompt. When a prompt works, keep it. Build a small library and your whole team gets faster, not just one person.

Two more habits for teams

4. Start with one real task, not a big plan.

Pick the one task your team likes least. Maybe it is writing recap emails or cleaning up notes after calls. Try AI on that one thing first. A small, real win beats a long strategy deck.

5. Share wins out loud.

Give the team five minutes in a weekly meeting to show one thing that worked. People copy what they see their coworkers do. This one habit does more for adoption than another email from leadership.

What a good first month looks like

Keep the goal simple. By the end of the first month, every person on the team should have one task they now do faster with AI, and one saved prompt they can hand to a teammate. That is it. Small habits, repeated, are what turn a login into a skill. As a leader, you can check it with two questions: who is using AI every week, and what did it save them?

So which company are you running?

Think back to the company at the start, where two people used AI every day and everyone else quietly went back to the old way. From the outside, that company looked just like one where AI is working. That is the hard part for a leader: you cannot fix what you cannot see.
