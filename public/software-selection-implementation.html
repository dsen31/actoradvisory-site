Finding and implementing the best CRM or ERP for a business can be a complex and challenging process. These systems are examples of the software decisions a business may need help evaluating.

Common selection and implementation challenges

  1. Understanding the benefits

    Many businesses may not fully understand the benefits of a CRM or ERP system, which can make it difficult to justify the investment. Without a clear understanding of how the system can improve business processes and outcomes, businesses may be hesitant to invest in a new system.

  2. Complex requirements

    Implementing a CRM or ERP system can be complex, particularly for businesses with complex processes or requirements. Finding a system that meets all of the business's needs may require custom development or integration work.

  3. Internal resources

    Implementation demands time from IT staff, business analysts, and the people who will use the system. Limited internal capacity can make introducing new software more difficult.

  4. Integration challenges

    Implementing a CRM or ERP system often requires integrating with other systems and processes. This can be challenging when the business has legacy systems or custom software that is difficult to integrate with.

  5. Resistance to change

    Implementation often requires significant changes to business processes and workflows. Employees may be resistant, particularly when they are accustomed to existing systems or processes.

  6. Cost

    Implementation can be expensive, particularly for small or mid-sized businesses with limited budgets. The cost may make the investment difficult to justify.

From selection to adoption

Finding and implementing the best CRM or ERP requires a careful process: identifying business needs, conducting research, evaluating vendors, conducting demos, planning implementation, testing and training, and monitoring and refining the system.
