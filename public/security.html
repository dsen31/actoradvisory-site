Protect your physical business.

Physical security for businesses refers to measures put in place to protect physical assets such as buildings, equipment, and inventory from theft, vandalism, or other physical threats.

Physical security measures

  1. Access control systems

    These systems control who can enter a building or specific areas within a building. Examples include keycard readers, biometric scanners, and PIN code locks.

  2. Surveillance cameras

    Cameras can monitor and record activity in and around a building. They can help deter criminal activity, provide evidence in the event of a crime, and help monitor employee activity.

  3. Alarm systems

    These systems can alert authorities or business owners in the event of a break-in or other security breach.

Why businesses consider physical security

  1. Protecting assets

    Physical security measures can help protect valuable equipment, inventory, and intellectual property from theft or damage.

  2. Employee safety

    Employee safety is part of a business's overall physical security strategy.

  3. Compliance

    Some industries may be subject to regulatory requirements for physical security measures. Businesses may need to implement specific measures to comply with these requirements.

  4. Reputation

    A security breach can damage a business's reputation and lead to a loss of customer trust. Physical security measures can help demonstrate a commitment to customer safety and security.

Physical security can support asset protection, employee safety, regulatory requirements, and reputation. For digital threats, explore the separate Cybersecurity service page.
